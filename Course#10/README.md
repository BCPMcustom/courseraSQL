# Mint Classics — Inventory & Warehouse Optimization Study

**A SQL-based business analysis project | Coursera**

[View Project Repository](https://github.com/BCPMcustom/courseraSQL/tree/main/Course%2310)

## Project Overview

The Mint Classics Company is a fictional retailer of classic model cars and related vehicles. Faced with the possibility of closing a warehouse, the company needs to understand its existing inventory, sales activity, and warehouse utilization before making a decision.

This project investigates the relationship between inventory levels, product demand, and warehouse operations using SQL and relational database analysis. The objective is to identify opportunities to reorganize inventory, reduce unnecessary stock, and improve operational efficiency without compromising the company's ability to serve customers.

Rather than treating warehouse closure as an isolated cost-cutting exercise, the study approaches it as a data-analysis problem: **What does the available data tell us about inventory allocation, product performance, and the feasibility of consolidating storage?**

## Business Objectives

- **Inventory analysis:** Examine stock levels and the distribution of products across warehouse facilities.
- **Sales and demand:** Investigate order history and product-level sales activity to identify potential mismatches between inventory and demand.
- **Warehouse utilization:** Evaluate inventory distribution and the operational considerations involved in consolidating storage.
- **Data-driven recommendations:** Translate query results into practical considerations for inventory management and warehouse optimization.

## Technical Approach

The study uses SQL to explore a relational database, investigate business questions, and develop an evidence-based view of inventory operations.

Key analytical techniques include:

- Relational queries and table joins to connect relevant business data.
- Filtering, aggregation, and grouping to summarize inventory and sales activity.
- Common Table Expressions (CTEs) to organize complex analytical logic into more manageable stages.
- Query refinement to improve readability, reduce unnecessary complexity, and make analytical intent easier to follow.
- Examination of inventory quantities, sales patterns, and warehouse-level measures to support operational recommendations.

The project also reflects an important distinction in analytical work: a query that executes successfully is not necessarily a query that represents the business correctly. Results must be checked against the underlying relationships and the question being investigated.

## Analytical Findings

The investigation considers several dimensions of warehouse and inventory performance:

1. **Inventory distribution:** Understanding which products and product lines occupy warehouse capacity.
2. **Inventory versus demand:** Identifying products whose stock levels warrant closer examination in relation to observed sales.
3. **Warehouse consolidation:** Assessing the implications of redistributing inventory and the conditions that would need to be satisfied before a facility could be closed.
4. **Operational and financial considerations:** Examining how stock allocation and inventory investment can inform decisions about the use of warehouse resources.

The findings provide an analytical basis for evaluating inventory reorganization opportunities. Any actual closure decision would require additional operational validation, including physical storage capacity, handling requirements, fulfillment constraints, and associated costs.

## Conclusion

The Mint Classics study applies SQL to a practical business problem in which inventory decisions, warehouse utilization, and customer service must be considered together.

The central lesson is that effective data analysis involves more than extracting numbers from a database. It requires understanding the data model, validating the results, refining the queries, and interpreting the evidence in the context of the business decision.

This project demonstrates the application of relational SQL analysis to an inventory-management problem and the use of analytical reasoning to support operational decision-making.
